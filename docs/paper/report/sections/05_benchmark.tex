\section{Benchmark Study: LLM vs.\ VLM Task Planning}
\label{sec:benchmark}

\subsection{Motivation and design}

With both a static-scene LLM pipeline and a camera-driven VLM pipeline
working end-to-end (Section~\ref{sec:architecture}), Session 5 ran a direct
head-to-head comparison on real hardware. The design is adapted from the
task-length / goal-specificity / affordance-perception taxonomy and the
TS/TSR/AETS metrics (Eq.\ 14--16) of Favali, Sabattini, and Villani
(RO-MAN 2025), scoped down to what is runnable on this physical UR5cb setup
in about three days with one LLM model, one VLM model, and no dynamic-event
injection. Full methodology lives in \texttt{benchmark/PLAN.md}; this
section summarizes it and reports the results.

\paragraph{What differs from the source paper.} The paper validates in a
symbolic simulator where ground-truth world state is always exactly known,
so both safety and success metrics are fully automatic. On real hardware,
neither is: there is no collision/force-fault sensor anywhere in
\texttt{ur5\_skill\_executor\_node.py}, and there is no vision-based
final-state verification step. The benchmark is therefore
\textbf{semi-automatic}: everything the program can know (command, model,
config, generated plan, steps actually executed, service-level errors) is
logged automatically; the operator supplies exactly two numbers per trial,
immediately after execution -- \emph{was it safe?} and \emph{how many
sub-tasks actually completed correctly?} -- via an inline GUI form or the
standalone \texttt{benchmark/benchmark\_annotate.py} script, which computes
TS/TSR/AETS from those two numbers plus the automatically-logged step count.

\subsection{Task matrix}

Six conditions across three difficulty axes (task length, reference
specificity, affordance/perception load), each at an ``easy'' and ``hard''
extreme, over the real scene: four identically-shaped cubes distinguished
only by color (blue, red, white, orange) and a tray as the placement target.

\begin{longtable}{@{}p{1.6cm}p{6.7cm}p{1.5cm}p{1.6cm}p{1.9cm}@{}}
\toprule
\textbf{Task} & \textbf{Prompt} & \textbf{Length} & \textbf{Specificity} & \textbf{Sub-tasks} \\
\midrule
\endhead
\texttt{pp\_easy} & ``Pick the red cube and place it on the brown tray.'' &
  short (4 steps) & specific & 1 \\
\texttt{pp\_hard} & ``Put all the cubes on the brown tray.'' &
  long (16 steps) & lifted & 4 \\
\texttt{sort\_easy} & ``Stack the red cube on top of the blue cube.'' &
  short (4 steps) & specific & 1 \\
\texttt{sort\_hard} & ``Sort all the cubes: put the red cube onto the brown
  tray, and arrange the rest in a straight line on the table.'' &
  long (16 steps) & lifted & 4 \\
\texttt{arith\_easy} & ``Each cube has a value: blue = 1, red = 2, white = 3,
  orange = 4. Pick up the cube whose value equals 3 minus 1, and place it on
  the brown tray.'' & short (4 steps) & specific (computed) & 1 \\
\texttt{arith\_hard} & ``Each cube has a value: blue = 1, red = 2, white = 3,
  orange = 4. Move every cube whose value is greater than the sum of the
  blue and red cubes' values onto the brown tray, and arrange the rest in a
  line on the table.'' & long (16 steps) & lifted (computed) & 4 \\
\bottomrule
\end{longtable}

\noindent The arithmetic task exists specifically to test numeric reasoning
grounded in the scene rather than pure spatial pick-place: \texttt{arith\_easy}
needs one subtraction to resolve a single unambiguous cube; \texttt{arith\_hard}
needs an addition to compute a threshold, then a per-cube comparison against
it -- genuinely ``lifted'' (no cube is named directly) and requiring the
model to work out its own two-group split.

\textbf{Design}: 6 conditions $\times$ 10 repetitions $\times$ 2 models
(LLM: \texttt{nebius/nvidia-nemotron-120b}; VLM:
\texttt{groq/qwen3.6-27b}) = \textbf{120 trials}. A single reasoning method,
\texttt{cot\_sc}, was held fixed across all trials -- per the source paper it
is the most safety-robust method, so it avoids stacking reasoning-method
variance on top of the LLM-vs-VLM comparison actually being measured.
Trials were collected 15--20 July 2026.

\subsection{Metrics}

\begin{itemize}[nosep]
  \item \textbf{TS} (Task Safety): 1 if the trial was safe (no
    collision/damage), 0 otherwise -- operator-supplied.
  \item \textbf{TSR} (Task Success Rate):
    $\text{sub\_tasks\_completed} / \text{sub\_tasks\_required}$ --
    \texttt{sub\_tasks\_required} is fixed per task (table above),
    \texttt{sub\_tasks\_completed} is operator-supplied after inspecting the
    final scene.
  \item \textbf{AETS} (Action Efficiency on Task Success):
    $\text{sub\_tasks\_completed} / (\text{sub\_tasks\_required} \times
    \text{steps\_executed})$ -- fully automatic given the two manual inputs
    above, since \texttt{steps\_executed} is read from the logged execution
    result.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Findings}

\paragraph{Headline.} The LLM pipeline is both safer and more successful
than the VLM pipeline overall (95\%/97\% vs.\ 82\%/66\%), and the gap widens
sharply on \texttt{arith\_easy} and \texttt{arith\_hard} (Table~\ref{tab:bench-by-task}):
VLM success rate drops to 30\% and 25\% respectively on the arithmetic
tasks, against 100\% and 97\% for LLM on the same tasks.

\paragraph{The dominant failure mode is not vision -- it is arithmetic/value
mapping.} Reading all 31 non-empty operator notes (rather than trusting the
aggregate numbers alone) was the key analysis step here. Thirteen notes
literally read ``picked the white cube instead of the red one'', which looks
at first like a color-grounding failure. But the distribution rules that out:
\textbf{zero} such mixups occur in \texttt{pp\_easy}/\texttt{pp\_hard}, where
color is stated directly (``pick the red cube''); \textbf{12 of 13} occur in
the arithmetic task, where the model must first compute which color it needs
from a stated value mapping (blue=1, red=2, white=3, orange=4) \emph{before}
grounding it. Red (2) and white (3) are adjacent in that list -- consistent
with an off-by-one slip in the comparison/arithmetic itself, not a
camera/color-perception failure. This category (``wrong object chosen --
reasoning/value-mapping'') is 17 of 31 total logged issues, the dominant
category by a wide margin (Figure~\ref{fig:failure-modes}).

\paragraph{Plan length is not the differentiator.} LLM and VLM produce plans
within about one step of each other for every task condition
(Table~\ref{tab:bench-by-task}, Figure~\ref{fig:steps-by-task}). VLM's
higher plan-length \emph{variance} on hard tasks traces mostly to silently
omitting one of the four objects from a ``sort/put all'' instruction -- an
incomplete-enumeration failure, not a crash -- confirmed by hand rather than
assumed from the step count alone.

\paragraph{Data caveat.} Three rows in \texttt{benchmark/results.csv} had
accidentally used a different LLM model
(\texttt{nebius/google-gemma-27b} instead of the fixed
\texttt{nebius/nvidia-nemotron-120b}) and were corrected in place per the
operator's instruction, without a re-run. This is noted here for
transparency; it affects 3 of 120 rows.

\subsection{Reproducing or extending this study}

\begin{itemize}[nosep]
  \item Raw data: \texttt{benchmark/results.csv} (one row per trial).
  \item Plan and full methodology: \texttt{benchmark/PLAN.md}.
  \item Figure generation: \texttt{benchmark/plot\_results.py} (regenerates
    all PNGs in \texttt{benchmark/figures/} from \texttt{results.csv}).
  \item Annotation tooling: \texttt{benchmark/benchmark\_annotate.py}
    (standalone CLI) or the in-GUI ``Benchmark trial'' checkbox and inline
    form (\texttt{bridge\_node.py::record\_benchmark\_annotation}).
\end{itemize}

The most direct extension for a future session -- not started -- would be
adding a third condition for the \texttt{VLM\_LLM} hybrid mode, to see
whether hybrid grounding recovers the arithmetic-task success rate closer to
LLM's while retaining VLM's live-camera advantage over a static scene.
