\subsection{Design}

With the deterministic grounding layer of \S\ref{sec:grounding-layer} in
place, we ran a 120-trial real-hardware study comparing a static-scene
LLM planner against a camera-grounded VLM planner, anchored to the
task/environment complexity taxonomy and TS/TSR/AETS metrics of Favali
et al.~\cite{favaliRAS}, adapted to a physical rig. Six task conditions
crossed three difficulty axes, task length, reference specificity, and
affordance/perception load, each easy and hard, over a real scene of
four color-distinguished cubes and a tray: \code{pp\_easy}/\code{pp\_hard}
(pick-and-place, one vs.\ four objects), \code{sort\_easy}/\code{sort\_hard}
(stacking and sorting), and \code{arith\_easy}/\code{arith\_hard} (the
object must be \emph{computed} from a stated numeric mapping rather than
named, isolating reasoning load from perception load). Each condition
ran 10 times per model, 120 trials total, with a single reasoning method
(chain-of-thought with self-consistency) held fixed, the most
safety-robust strategy per~\cite{favaliRAS} and one that avoids stacking
reasoning-method variance on top of the comparison under test. Both
planners use the same underlying model (\code{kimi-k2.6}), text-only and
image-grounded respectively, so grounding modality is the only variable
isolated between arms.\footnote{An earlier run used different models per
modality; the VLM-specific one was later withdrawn from its provider's
catalog, forcing a full re-run on a single unified model instead.}
\fb{The accompanying video presents the physical rig and representative
trials from all six conditions, including a multi-object placement
collision as described in \S\ref{sec:case-collision}, together with the
operator's annotation step performed immediately after each trial.}

\subsection{Metrics, adapted for physical ground truth}

Favali et al.'s TS (task safety), TSR (task success rate), and AETS
(action efficiency on task success) are fully automatic in a symbolic
simulator, where whether an action ``damages the environment'' or a
sub-task ``completes'' is always exactly derivable from simulator state.
Our real UR5cb has no collision or force-fault sensor anywhere in its
control path and no vision-based verification of the final scene, so we
adapt the same three metrics to a \textbf{semi-automatic} protocol:
everything the system can know automatically is logged by the debug
harness of \S\ref{sec:grounding-layer} for every trial, and the operator
supplies exactly two numbers immediately after, was it safe and how many
sub-tasks completed, from which TS, TSR, and AETS are computed
identically to~\cite{favaliRAS}'s definitions.

At $n=10$ per cell, point estimates alone invite exactly the kind of
pushback a reviewer should raise, so every reported TS\% and TSR\% below
carries a 95\% Wilson score interval (TS is an exact per-trial
proportion; TSR pools successes over $\text{sub\_tasks\_required} \times
n$). One comparison was declared before any data was collected, not
chosen afterward to fit the numbers: a two-sided Fisher's exact test,
LLM vs.\ VLM, on \code{arith\_hard} full-trial success, at $\alpha=0.05$.
It is binarized per trial rather than pooled across a trial's four
sub-tasks, since those are not independent draws; one wrong decision
tends to misplace every object in the trial together, not each
independently. Everything else is reported descriptively only.

\subsection{The failure mode is computation, not perception}
\label{sec:computation-not-perception}

Reading all 22 non-empty operator failure notes from the 120 trials,
rather than trusting aggregates alone, was the key analysis step. Every
note describes either a collision between objects (10) or an
execution/grasp failure, a slipped grasp, a release too high, a dropped
object (12), during a multi-object task. \textbf{Zero} describe a wrong
object chosen for reasoning or value-mapping reasons, and zero an
incomplete enumeration (Figure~\ref{fig:failure-modes}). This happens in
\textbf{both} arms: the LLM contributes 4 of the 22 notes (all on
\code{arith\_hard}), the VLM the remaining 18, spread across all three
multi-object conditions roughly in proportion to trial counts. The
failure recurs regardless of which planner grounded the scene, direct
confirmation that Case 6 (\S\ref{sec:case-collision}) is this taxonomy's
most consequential open item, not a peculiarity of one pipeline.

Unlike the closed bugs of \S\ref{sec:taxonomy}, this result is not a

Case 6 is itself one of the six geometry-grounding bugs of

dominant one once the other five are closed.
