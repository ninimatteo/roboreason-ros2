We presented \harness{} (\harnesslong{}), an execution harness
that turns a two-layer LLM/VLM Sense-Plan-Act pipeline into a
three-layer one. \rev{The planner keeps the symbolic decisions;
\harness{} recomputes each action's physical quantities from sensor
measurements or from actions already committed in the plan; the
executor turns the corrected plan into motion.} The harness is
motivated by a structured investigation of pick-and-place on a real
UR5cb manipulator, which surfaced six recurring failure modes,
invisible to symbolic simulation by construction. The failures arise
even under perfect perception, so the problem lies in reasoning about
physical quantities, not in perceiving the scene, and the harness
accordingly operates at the execution level. \rev{By applying
\harness{}, we resolve five of the six failure modes.} A 120-trial
real-hardware benchmark, using a single unified model for both
grounding modalities, validates the harness and shows the sixth
failure, a multi-object placement collision, as the dominant residual
failure in both \rev{implementations}. \rev{Resolving} that failure's
dynamic, trajectory-level half as a new skill, and a depth-verified
treatment of grasp width, are the most direct next steps. More
broadly, we propose the geometry-grounding audit as a standard
companion to task-complexity benchmarking for any LLM/VLM planner
headed to physical hardware.
